There is not one Claude. There are a few, and picking the right one for the job is the biggest lever you have on what this costs you.

Two minutes here saves you a lot of frustration later.

---

**THERE ARE SEVERAL CLAUDES**

They are not different products. They are the same idea, built at different sizes.

Bigger models reason better. They hold more of a hard problem in view at once, they catch nuance, and they need less correcting. They also run slower and cost more.

Smaller models are faster and much cheaper. On simple jobs they are just as good, because a simple job does not need deep reasoning. Formatting a list does not benefit from a genius.

Here is the ladder, most advanced and most expensive at the top. Each one is compared to the one directly below it, so you can see the step you are taking each time you move up.

**FABLE**

The most advanced and the most expensive. About twice the price of Opus.

Save it for the hardest thinking you do. If being wrong would be expensive, this is the one.

Hand it: your quarterly or annual plan. Designing your offer from scratch. A launch plan you are going to commit real money to. Untangling something genuinely broken that you have already failed to fix twice.

**OPUS**

The next step down, and about five times the price of Sonnet.

This is your workhorse for anything that matters. Real thinking, real judgment, anything a customer will read.

Hand it: sales copy. Your offer doc. A webinar or VSL script. YouTube scripts. Belief-shift work. Planning documents. Working through a decision you are stuck on.

**SONNET**

The middle, and about twice the price of Haiku.

Use it for most everyday content, especially when you are working from a pattern that already exists and you are not inventing the approach.

Hand it: community posts. Reels or carousels from a brief. Emails against a template you have already proven. Editing and tightening something you already wrote. Routine research.

**HAIKU**

The smallest and cheapest.

Mechanical work with a clear spec and low downside if it is not perfect.

Hand it: formatting. Moving or renaming files. Pulling numbers. Updating a list. Tagging. Simple lookups.

---

**THE MISTAKE RUNS BOTH WAYS**

This is the part most people never learn.

Put Fable or Opus on a filing job and you have paid many times over for a result Haiku would have nailed. Same output, a fraction of the price, and you would not have been able to tell the difference.

Go the other way and it is worse. Put Haiku on your sales page and you get sales-page-shaped writing that does not sell. You did not save money, you wasted the whole attempt and now you have to do it again.

The rule: match the model to the job. Not the cheapest, not the best. The right one.

---

**HOW THIS WORKS WITH YOUR SUBSCRIPTION**

Most of you are on a Claude subscription, Pro or Max. Here is what that actually means.

You pay a flat monthly fee. Claude Code is included in it. You are not billed per message, and there is no meter running that can surprise you with a bill at the end of the month.

What you have instead is a usage limit. Your plan gives you a certain amount of work in a rolling window. If you use it all, Claude pauses and tells you when it resets. It stops. It does not start charging you.

So the real question is not "how much am I spending." It is "how much can I get done before I hit the limit."

That reframes everything. Every time you put Opus on a job Sonnet could do, you are not spending extra money, you are spending your day. You are burning through your allowance several times faster for no extra quality, and then sitting there waiting for a reset.

If you are on the API instead of a subscription, you genuinely are billed per word, and all of this matters more, not less. Most of you would know if that were you.

---

**THE FOUR HABITS**

**One. Match the model to the job.**

The biggest lever, by a distance. Type /model to switch. If you are unsure, just ask: "which model should I be on for this?" and say what you are about to do.

A good rhythm: strategy and customer-facing writing on Opus, content on Sonnet, admin on Haiku.

**Two. Start a new conversation for a new task.**

Claude re-reads the entire conversation every time you send a message. That means a long thread costs more with every single turn, even when your question is short.

Staying in one thread all day is the most common expensive mistake people make. When you switch to something genuinely different, start fresh. It is cheaper and the answers get sharper.

**Three. Keep your CLAUDE.md good.**

When Claude already knows your business, it gets things right the first time instead of after four corrections. Four corrections cost four times what one good answer costs.

The work you did filling in your business brain is already saving you money every day.

**Four. Be specific.**

This one is worth more than the other three combined.

"Write me a three-sentence Instagram caption about morning routines" gets you something usable immediately.

"Write me something" gets a guess, then a rewrite, then another rewrite. Three attempts to get where a clear ask lands in one.

Tell Claude what it is, who it is for, how long, and what it should do.

---

**THE MINDSET**

The goal is not to be cheap. It is to not waste.

You are not trying to squeeze the smallest possible amount out of this. You are trying to avoid paying ten times over for work that did not need it, and avoid burning three attempts on a request you could have made clearly the first time.

Match the model to the job. Start fresh when you switch tasks. Ask clearly.

That is the whole game.
